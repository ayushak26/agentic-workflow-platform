1. Excellence

1.1 Objectives and ambition

AGRO-THRIVE will develop and validate a decision-intelligence framework that helps agricultural actors compare bioeconomy opportunities against food security, climate, biodiversity, soil and farm-income objectives. The framework will connect parcel-level evidence, regional scenarios and European policy requirements without presenting one model output as a universal answer.

The proposal has four measurable objectives:

  1. Create a harmonised evidence base for agricultural biomass availability, quality, logistics and environmental safeguards.
  2. Develop transparent feasibility and multi-objective optimisation models.
  3. Validate the approach across ten pilot case studies and different crop, climate and value-chain contexts.
  4. Produce adoption-ready decision services, methods, training and policy recommendations.

1.2 State of the art and progress beyond it

Remote sensing, field observations and operational data can be combined to improve spatial biomass assessment [1][2]. The proposal will advance beyond single-model prediction by preserving source versions, uncertainty, applicable conditions and claim-level evidence links. Every scientific claim released to a proposal drafter must have an exact full-text passage and page locator.

1.3 Methodology

The methodology follows an evidence-first sequence: call requirements are decomposed, proposal claims are atomised, source routes are selected, candidate records are discovered, full text is acquired, passages are retrieved and claim-source pairs are verified. Candidate titles, abstracts and search snippets never change a claim's verification status.

Stage Main output Hard gate
Claim preparation Atomic, typed claims Compound critical claims are split
Discovery Candidate-source register Candidate is not evidence
Full-text acquisition Immutable document version Content hash and page text

2. Impact

2.1 Pathways towards impact

The project will connect activities to results, users, adoption mechanisms, outcomes and longer-term impacts. Each proposed KPI will retain its baseline, target, unit, measurement source, owner and target date. Project targets will be labelled as future commitments and will not be presented as achieved results.

2.2 Measures to maximise impact

Dissemination, exploitation and communication will be planned for farmers, cooperatives, industrial users, technical providers, researchers, policymakers and auditors. The final plan will define foreground ownership, access rights, open-science routes, exploitable results, target channels and responsible partners.

3. Quality and efficiency of implementation

3.1 Work plan and resources

The work plan will combine project management, evidence and data foundations, AI/ML modelling, decision intelligence, platform integration, pilot validation and impact activities. Dependencies will be explicit so that modelling results cannot be treated as deployment-ready before data-quality and validation gates have passed.
