# Product Selection Guide — Choosing a Dura Series Pump Configuration

## 1. How to Use This Guide

Select a pump-head material and diaphragm combination in three steps:
identify the chemical and concentration, check the Chemical Compatibility
Guide for approved materials, then confirm the flow/pressure duty falls
within the Dura 25's operating range in the Product Manual.

## 2. Recommended Configurations by Duty

| Duty | Recommended pump head | Recommended diaphragm | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sodium hypochlorite, 10–15%, water treatment | PVDF | PTFE | Do not substitute PVC or EPDM |
| Sodium hypochlorite, below 10%, below 30°C | PVDF or PVC | PTFE | PVDF preferred for longer service life |
| Sulfuric acid, up to 30% | PVC | PTFE | PVDF also acceptable at higher concentration |
| Sodium hydroxide, up to 20% | PVC, PVDF or 316 stainless | PTFE or EPDM | Least restrictive duty in this guide |
| Ferric chloride | PVDF | PTFE | Avoid PVC and stainless |

## 3. Decision Checklist

1. Is the chemical and concentration listed in the Chemical Compatibility
   Guide? If not, do not proceed without a compatibility test.
2. Does the required flow rate fall within 0.5–25 L/h? If a higher flow
   rate is needed, escalate to the Dura 100 series instead of over-driving
   a Dura 25 past its rated stroke frequency.
3. Is the ambient temperature within the compatible range for the selected
   material at the required concentration (see the Chemical Compatibility
   Guide's temperature notes)?
4. Has the diaphragm material been checked against Section 4 of the
   Chemical Compatibility Guide, not assumed from the pump head material
   alone?

## 4. Common Selection Mistakes

- Choosing 316 stainless steel for chlorine-based chemicals because it is
  the "premium" material — stainless is worse than PVDF for sodium
  hypochlorite due to chloride pitting.
- Reusing a PVC configuration from a lower-concentration duty when moving
  to a higher-concentration or higher-temperature duty of the same
  chemical.
- Selecting EPDM diaphragms by default without checking chemical
  compatibility — EPDM is only appropriate for the alkaline duties in this
  guide, never for sodium hypochlorite.
