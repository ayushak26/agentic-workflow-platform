# Dura 25 Metering Pump — Product Manual

## 1. Overview

The Dura 25 is a diaphragm metering pump designed for continuous, low-flow
dosing of industrial process chemicals. It is rated for flow rates between
0.5 and 25 liters per hour at pressures up to 10 bar. The wetted pump head
is available in three material variants: PVC, PVDF and 316 stainless steel,
selected according to the chemical being dosed (see the Chemical
Compatibility Guide for material selection).

## 2. Installation

1. Mount the pump on a level, vibration-free surface within 1.5 meters of
   the dosing point.
2. Connect the suction line to the bottom of the chemical supply tank,
   using a foot valve to prevent siphoning when the pump is idle.
3. Connect the discharge line to the injection point through a pressure
   relief valve rated for at least 1.5x the pump's maximum discharge
   pressure.
4. Prime the pump head before first use by running it at maximum stroke
   length with the discharge line disconnected until liquid flows freely
   from the pump head.

## 3. Operating Parameters

| Parameter | Range |
|---|---|
| Flow rate | 0.5 – 25 L/h |
| Discharge pressure | up to 10 bar |
| Stroke length | 0 – 100% (manual dial) |
| Stroke frequency | 10 – 180 strokes/min |
| Ambient temperature | 0°C – 45°C |
| Wetted materials | PVC, PVDF, 316 stainless steel |

## 4. Chemical Dosing with Sodium Hypochlorite

The Dura 25 with a PVDF pump head, PTFE diaphragm and ceramic ball valves is
approved for continuous dosing of sodium hypochlorite solutions up to 15%
concentration. Do not use the PVC variant for sodium hypochlorite above 10%
concentration at temperatures over 30°C — PVC degrades faster than PVDF
under those conditions and the warranty does not cover wetted-parts failure
outside the approved configuration. See the Chemical Compatibility Guide
for the full compatibility matrix and the Product Selection Guide for how
to choose the right pump-head variant for a given duty.

## 5. Maintenance Schedule

- **Weekly**: Visually inspect the pump head and tubing for leaks or
  discoloration.
- **Monthly**: Check the diaphragm torque and re-tighten the pump head bolts
  to the specified value (see Section 3 of the Troubleshooting Guide for
  torque values by pump-head material).
- **Every 6 months**: Replace the diaphragm and inspect the ball valves for
  chemical attack, regardless of visible wear.
- **Annually**: Full disassembly, inspection of all wetted parts, and
  recalibration of stroke length against measured flow.
