# Chemical Compatibility Guide — Dura Series Metering Pumps

## 1. Purpose

This guide lists the wetted-material compatibility of the Dura pump series
(including the Dura 25) with common industrial process chemicals. Selecting
an incompatible material shortens diaphragm and seal life and voids the
pump's chemical-attack warranty.

## 2. Compatibility Matrix

| Chemical | Concentration | PVC | PVDF | 316 Stainless |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sodium hypochlorite | up to 10% | Compatible below 30°C | Compatible up to 15%, 45°C | Not recommended (pitting risk) |
| Sodium hypochlorite | 10–15% | Not recommended | Compatible up to 45°C | Not recommended |
| Sulfuric acid | up to 30% | Compatible | Compatible | Not recommended |
| Sulfuric acid | above 50% | Not recommended | Compatible | Not recommended |
| Sodium hydroxide | up to 20% | Compatible | Compatible | Compatible |
| Ferric chloride | any | Not recommended | Compatible | Not recommended |
| Potable water / brine | any | Compatible | Compatible | Compatible |

## 3. Notes on Sodium Hypochlorite

Sodium hypochlorite is one of the most common dosing chemicals in water
treatment and its compatibility is temperature- and concentration-dependent:

- **PVDF** pump heads are the recommended default for sodium hypochlorite
  dosing above 10% concentration, and remain compatible up to 15%
  concentration at temperatures up to 45°C.
- **PVC** pump heads are acceptable only below 10% concentration and below
  30°C; above that combination, PVC degrades faster than the diaphragm
  replacement interval assumes, and leaks can develop between scheduled
  maintenance visits.
- **316 stainless steel** is not recommended for sodium hypochlorite at any
  concentration due to chloride pitting corrosion; use PVDF instead even
  where stainless is otherwise the default choice for a duty.

## 4. Elastomer and Diaphragm Compatibility

PTFE diaphragms are compatible with every chemical in the matrix above and
are the standard fitment on PVDF pump heads. EPDM diaphragms must not be
used with sodium hypochlorite at any concentration — EPDM degrades within
weeks of continuous exposure and is only rated for the alkaline chemicals in
Section 2 (e.g. sodium hydroxide).

## 5. When Compatibility Data Is Missing

If a chemical is not listed in the matrix above, treat it as incompatible
with all wetted materials until confirmed otherwise by material safety data
and a compatibility test, not by extrapolation from a chemically similar
listed substance.
