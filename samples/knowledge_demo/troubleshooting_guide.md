# Troubleshooting Guide — Dura Series Metering Pumps

## 1. Pump Runs But No Flow

1. Check that the foot valve is submerged and not air-locked; re-prime the
   pump head per Section 2 of the Product Manual.
2. Inspect the suction and discharge ball valves for chemical scaling —
   this is common with ferric chloride and hard-water dosing.
3. Confirm the stroke length dial is not set to 0%.
4. If flow is still absent after priming and valve inspection, the
   diaphragm may be ruptured; replace it per Section 5 of the Product
   Manual regardless of the maintenance schedule.

## 2. Reduced or Erratic Flow

- **Air in the suction line**: tighten all suction-side fittings and check
  the foot valve strainer for partial blockage.
- **Worn ball valve seats**: chemical attack on ball valve seats causes
  back-flow during the suction stroke, reducing net output; this is the
  most common cause of erratic (rather than absent) flow.
- **Viscosity change**: a chemical supply that has crystallized or thickened
  at low ambient temperature will reduce achievable flow even with a fully
  functional pump head.

## 3. Pump Head Bolt Torque Values

| Pump head material | Torque (Nm) |
|---|---|
| PVC | 4.5 |
| PVDF | 6.0 |
| 316 stainless steel | 8.5 |

Over-torquing a PVC pump head is the leading cause of hairline cracks
around the bolt bosses; always use a calibrated torque wrench, never an
impact driver.

## 4. Visible Leak at the Pump Head

1. Stop the pump and isolate the chemical supply immediately.
2. Inspect the diaphragm for chemical attack consistent with an
   incompatible material choice — cross-check against the Chemical
   Compatibility Guide before ordering a replacement of the same material.
3. If the diaphragm shows chemical attack rather than mechanical wear, the
   pump head material itself may be wrong for the duty; escalate to the
   Product Selection Guide before re-installing the same configuration.

## 5. Unexpected Diaphragm Failure Before the 6-Month Interval

Early diaphragm failure almost always indicates a chemical compatibility
mismatch (see the Chemical Compatibility Guide) or over-torqued pump head
bolts (see Section 3 above), not a manufacturing defect. Confirm both
before requesting a warranty replacement.
